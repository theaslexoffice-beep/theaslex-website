The Practice

Technical depth. Strategic advocacy.

A specialised practice at the intersection of tax, securities and corporate law.

The AS Lex represents clients across India in income tax, GST, securities regulation, corporate governance and economic-offence matters, appearing before tribunals, regulatory authorities and superior courts for both revenue and private interests.

The practice combines technical precision with strategic advocacy, drawing on accounting, finance and law to handle complex litigation: reassessment proceedings, penalty disputes, SEBI enforcement, NCLT matters and PMLA cases.

i.

Both sides of the dispute

Experience on revenue-side and assessee-side matters gives a complete view of how a case will be argued, and how it will be answered.

ii.

Numbers, read closely

Grounded in accounting and finance. Balance sheets, ledgers and valuation reports are read as evidence, not as annexures.

iii.

Drafting as a craft

Every submission is built for a busy bench: precise, fully sourced, and structured around the decision it asks for.

Approach

How a matter is run

A disciplined method from the first reading of the record to the final hearing.

01

Diagnose

The record, the timeline and the jurisdiction are examined first: limitation, sanction, authority and service, before the merits.

02

Strategise

Threshold objections and the merits are sequenced so that the strongest ground leads and every fallback is preserved.

03

Draft

Grounds, submissions and paper books are written to be read, with every proposition tied to the record and to authority.

04

Represent

Focused oral advocacy before the bench, with the numbers at hand and an answer ready for the other side.

The Wrong Officer Signed the Extension: ITAT Delhi Voids a Rs. 208 Crore Assessment in Patanjali Ayurved

A special audit under Section 142(2A) needs the Commissioner's approval. An extension of time to complete it does not — under the proviso to Section 142(2C) that power belongs to the Assessing Officer alone. When the Principal Commissioner granted the extension instead, the Delhi Tribunal held the assessment void ab initio, and the merits never came into play.

A Valuation Reference the AO Was Never Allowed to Make: Gujarat HC Quashes Reassessment Over the Pre-2012 Section 55A Rule

The Gujarat High Court quashes a Section 148 reassessment built entirely on a DVO report the AO had no power to obtain. Under the pre-1 July 2012 Section 55A, a valuation reference was permissible only where the assessee's declared value was less than fair market value — not more — and the 2012 amendment cannot be applied backwards to save it.

Net Winnings, Not Gross: Hyderabad ITAT Deletes a Rs 3.54 Crore Online-Gaming Addition under Section 115BB
